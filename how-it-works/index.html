The rules of the game

How it works.

Predict when Baby Sipe will arrive—and whether it's a boy or a girl. Trade your predictions, watch the odds move, and compete for Sipe Points.

The short version

A prediction market, minus the real money.

01 / START

Everyone gets §1,000.

Every player starts with §1,000 in imaginary Sipe Points. There's nothing to deposit and no actual money at stake.

02 / PREDICT

Buy the outcome you believe in.

Pick Boy or Girl, or choose a date and predict On or before, On or after, or Exact day. You can buy or sell shares as the odds move.

03 / WIN

Sell early—or wait for the birth.

Trade shares at any time. At settlement, each winning share pays §1. Losing shares pay §0. Most points wins the bragging rights.

Think of the price as the crowd's odds. A Boy share trading at §0.600 means the market is pricing Boy at roughly 60%. The price changes as people trade—not because a moderator chooses the odds.
What you can predict

Two markets. One baby.

💙 🩷 Boy or Girl

Buy Boy or Girl shares. Only the winning outcome pays §1 per share. Both prices always add up to §1.

📅 Birthday

Choose a date and one of three inclusive contracts. On or before Jan 27 wins Jan 27 or earlier; On or after Jan 27 wins Jan 27 or later; Exact Jan 27 wins only Jan 27. All three share the same birthday odds.

Under the hood

Now, the math.

Sipe Exchange uses a Logarithmic Market Scoring Rule (LMSR). It's an automated market maker: the exchange always quotes a price, so you don't have to find someone to take the other side of a trade.

01 / PRICING
Prices are probabilities.

The market tracks a quantity qᵢ for each possible outcome. A mathematical function called softmax turns those quantities into prices:

pᵢ = exp(qᵢ / b) / Σⱼ exp(qⱼ / b)

pᵢ is the share price and implied probability of outcome i. b controls how easily trades move prices; it's currently fixed at 2,600. For Boy/Girl, p(Boy) + p(Girl) = 1.

02 / EXECUTION
The price moves as you trade.

Every purchase changes the inventory, so buying a lot of shares gets progressively more expensive. LMSR calculates the exact price of the whole trade with a cost function:

C(q) = b × ln(Σⱼ exp(qⱼ / b))
Trade cost = C(q after) − C(q before)

Buying increases the relevant q values; selling decreases them. The cost difference is what you pay to buy, or what the market pays you when you sell.

Example: With Boy initially at §0.500, spending §100 buys about 196.30 Boy shares and lifts its price to roughly §0.519. You don't get 200 shares because your buy moves the price along the way.
03 / BIRTHDAY ODDS
Dates are cumulative.

Underneath the birthday market are 31 probability buckets: the days in the January 13–February 11, 2027 window, plus a later-birth bucket. The earliest bucket also includes dates before January 13. Each contract uses the same daily probabilities:

P(On or before d) = Σᵢ≤d pᵢ
P(On or after d) = Σᵢ≥d pᵢ
P(Exact day d) = p_d

Buying On or before Jan 27 adds inventory to all outcomes through Jan 27. Buying On or after Jan 27 adds inventory to Jan 27 and all later outcomes. Buying Exact Jan 27 adds inventory only to that day. Everyone trades against the same LMSR market maker.

The two inclusive sides overlap on the selected day: their prices add up to §1 plus the exact-day price. Because the first probability bucket includes January 13 and earlier dates, the market offers Exact day and On or after contracts starting January 14.

04 / LIQUIDITY
Market depth is fixed.

The parameter b = 2,600 controls price sensitivity. A smaller b means a single trade can swing the odds dramatically; a larger b makes the market steadier.

b = 2,600

Liquidity is fixed today. New participants receiving their initial §1,000 do not automatically change existing prices or expand market depth.

05 / YOUR BALANCE
What your shares are worth.

Your portfolio combines unspent points with your shares valued at the latest market prices:

Portfolio = Cash + Σ(shares × current price)

The gain or loss below each position compares its current price to your average purchase cost:

Unrealized P/L = shares × (current price − average cost)

Those are estimated values if you marked every share at today's prices, not guaranteed sale proceeds. A large sale would move the market as it executes.

06 / SETTLEMENT
The result decides the winner.

After the birth, each correctly predicted share pays §1. Each incorrect share pays §0. Your final score is your unspent balance plus all winning share payouts.

Final points = Cash + winning shares × §1

For example, if you paid an average §0.400 for 100 winning shares, you spent §40 and receive §100 at settlement: a §60 gain.
